# Field-rule pilot — 2026-09-25

This was a live, nine-request direct-BYOK pilot against Jev with
`evals/field-rules.json --sample-per-label 1`. Only fictional examples were
sent. The key and submitted text are not included in this report.

| Rule | Positive case | Negative case | Ambiguous case |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `person_name` | `name-p01`: valid, 1.00 | `name-n01`: invalid, 0.97 | `name-a01`: valid, 0.81 |
| `product_description` | `product-p01`: valid, 1.00 | `product-n01`: invalid, 1.00 | `product-a01`: invalid, 0.85 |
| `address` | `address-p01`: valid, 0.99 | `address-n01`: invalid, 1.00 | `address-a01`: invalid, 0.93 |

The six labeled positive/negative decisions matched their labels; there were
no request errors. All three intentionally ambiguous inputs received a
decisive result rather than `uncertain`. This shows that the current 0.80
confidence threshold must **not** be treated as an ambiguity detector. Six
labeled examples are far too few to estimate accuracy or justify changing a
threshold. A full 90-case evaluation and application-specific cases remain
necessary before making any reliability claim.
